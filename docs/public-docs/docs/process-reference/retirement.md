# Phase 2: Retirement, migration and closure

Phase 2 ends the product's service or transfers it to a replacement. Entry requires an authorized retirement decision and an agreed disposition for development and release work already in progress. Operations continues through migration until the service can shut down safely.

Inputs include the retirement decision, running versions, release and operations records, assets and data, retention obligations, costs and remaining risks.

## Plan retirement

The product owner and responsible specialists agree on the reason for retirement, scope, replacement, user communication, schedule and exit criteria.

Check consumers and integrations, data migration, legal retention obligations, remaining cost and recovery needs. The plan identifies who is responsible for each part of the transition.

## Migrate data and shut down the service

The cloud platform engineer carries out approved migration, archival, retention or deletion and verifies the result. After the required support period, the service owner follows the agreed sequence to stop incoming work, schedules, credentials, access and environments.

Remove resources that incur cost, or transfer them with accepted ownership and budget. Check that integrations and consumers have completed their transition.

## Settle records and data obligations

The records and data steward checks repositories, backups, exports and other copies covered by the plan.

An archive may remain after service shutdown. Its receiving owner must accept responsibility for access control, budget, retention expiry and eventual deletion. Record and verify the transfer.

## Accept closure at G8

The process reviewer gathers the retirement and operational shutdown evidence. The sponsor and service owner, with the records and data steward, review closure for both processes.

Closure requires that no release remains live and that no service access, obligation or cost-bearing resource remains without an agreed disposition. Retained archives must have accepted ownership and controls.

The outputs are the retirement record, migration or data-disposition evidence, access and resource closure records, accepted archival responsibilities and lessons for later projects.
